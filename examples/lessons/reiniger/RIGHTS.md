# Rights: The silhouette film

The rule for this lesson: **cite the work, make original material, or use what is clearly public domain everywhere.**

- **Drawings and code.** Every shape in the lesson (the puppet, the hills, the reeds, the moon, the lightbox) is drawn in code for this lesson. None is copied from Lotte Reiniger's films or books.
- **The film.** The Adventures of Prince Achmed (1926) is cited, not reproduced. It is in the public domain in the United States, but Reiniger died in 1981, so her work is still protected in Germany and the EU: no frame, design or still is used here.
- **Textures.** The paper fibre, film grain and lamp mask are generated by the code (random noise and gradients).
- **Photographs.** None. A photograph for a backdrop must come from an author who died more than 100 years ago, from a faithful scan with no retouching or enhancement, and its source and licence are recorded here before it is used. (A separate lesson on image textures is planned.)

## The cover picture (and what is not on it)

- **The cover** shows the lesson's own puppet walking through its own set, drawn by the code of the last board (`LESSON.cover`). It is original.
- **A poster or a still of The Adventures of Prince Achmed is not used.** The film (1926) is in the public domain in the United States, but not
  everywhere: Reiniger died in 1981, so her work is protected in Germany and the rest of the EU until the end of 2051, and a poster or still shows
  her designs (and the poster artist has rights of their own). A picture may be *quoted* in the EU under narrow conditions (a lecture, with the source
  named, no more than the purpose needs), which is a legal argument and not a clear right, so by this lesson's rule (cite, make your own, or use what is
  clearly public domain everywhere) it stays out. To show her work, link to a copy whose source and rights you have checked, and say what to look at.
