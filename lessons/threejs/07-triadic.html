What it is

Each part hangs on a segment of the body: the shoulder-to-elbow bone, the torso, the head. A segment gives the part its position, direction and length; the part's thickness comes from your shoulder width, and the head's size from the distance between your ears. So the same costume fits a child, an adult, or a dancer far from the camera.

Schlemmer's Triadic Ballet (1922) dressed three dancers in geometry: spheres, spirals, discs and cones, in yellow, pink and black. These are studies in that spirit, not reconstructions of the costumes. A part on a joint the camera cannot see is hidden, not guessed.
